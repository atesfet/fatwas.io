Abstract

Adipose tissue plays a central role in metabolic and inflammatory homeostasis. Connecting routine clinical imaging with health records offers an opportunistic approach to characterize adipose tissue through large-scale quantitative phenotyping, revealing systemic disease mechanisms. We performed a phenome-wide association study linking CT-derived adipose metrics to electronic health record phenotypes in 22,710 patients’ abdominal CT scans from two tertiary care centers. Subcutaneous and visceral fat cross-sectional area indices (SFI, VFI) and densities (SFD, VFD) were derived using a validated deep learning pipeline and tested against 624 distinct phenotypes (2,496 tests across four markers). We identified 922 significant associations across 373 phenotypes, recapitulating established relationships with morbid obesity (SFI OR=1.60), type 2 diabetes (VFI OR=1.57), cirrhosis (VFD OR=3.56), and pancreatic cancer (VFD OR=2.15). Adipose area and density effects were inversely correlated in their direction of associations across the phenome (Pearson r = −0.80, p < 0.0001). They diverged in direction for 164 of the 178 phenotypes associated with both classes, indicating that CT density conveys composition-related physiology largely non-redundant with adipose quantity. When area and density were modelled jointly, density remained associated with disease independently of area for 351 of the 392 phenotypes with a joint adipose association, whereas area was independently informative for 136, and without density for only 6. These findings provide a hypothesis-generating atlas of adipose–disease associations in which adipose density emerges as a candidate biomarker of systemic physiology for replication and mechanistic study.

This website provides Manhattan and volcano plots of all 2,496 marker–phenotype tests, a searchable table of every association, the joint area–density atlas, and downloadable result files.

Study at a Glance

  • Cohort. 22,710 adults with a portal venous phase, contrast-enhanced abdominal CT (January 2013 – June 2024) at two tertiary care centres; earliest scan per patient.
  • CT markers. At the L3 vertebral level, a validated deep learning pipeline measured subcutaneous and visceral fat area indices (SFI, VFI; cm²/m) and densities (SFD, VFD; HU). Each marker was standardised to a sex-specific z-score, so odds ratios are per one standard deviation.
  • Phenotypes. ICD-9/ICD-10 codes from 1 year before to 3 months after the scan, mapped to phecodes (Phecode Map v1.2). Patients with ≥ 2 occurrences of a phecode were cases, 0 occurrences controls, and 1 occurrence excluded. Phenotypes with ≥ 200 cases and ≥ 200 controls were tested (624).
  • Models. Logistic regression per marker and phenotype, adjusted for age, sex, BMI, tube current, tube voltage and slice thickness. Significance: Bonferroni over all 2,496 tests, p < 0.05/2,496 = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ (red dashed line in the plots).
  • Joint area–density models. For each phenotype, all four markers in one model. Likelihood-ratio tests asked whether the markers carry any signal (4 df) and whether density adds to area, or area to density (2 df each). See the atlas below.
  • Limitation. The BMI covariate is the most recent value in the EHR, not a measurement at the time of the scan. All associations are cross-sectional and hypothesis-generating.

Website Usage Tips

Each plot shows all 624 tested phenotypes for that marker. Coloured points (by phenotype group) are Bonferroni-significant; grey points are not. The red dashed line is the Bonferroni threshold.

Hover over points for phenotype details, odds ratios and confidence intervals. Use the drag/zoom tools in the top-right of each plot to inspect dense regions.

Click a row, or its Focus button, in either table to highlight that phenotype across all plots. Plots in which the phenotype is not significant show a centred message.

The association table shows significant associations by default. Tick Show all 2,496 tests to include non-significant results.

Use Download Focus PDF to export a report with the focused plots and the phenotype's results for all four markers.

Use Open Discussion (GitHub) to open the phenotype discussion thread/search in this repository (GitHub account required to comment). This is not an "Issue" but a "Feature"!

Overall Results

Density accounted for 655 of the 922 significant associations and area for 267. Of the 373 phenotypes with at least one association, 187 were associated with density only, 8 with area only, and 178 with both. For 164 of those 178, area and density pointed in opposite directions, almost always lower area with higher density (156).

Joint Area–Density Atlas

Area and density are correlated, so a single-marker association with density could simply mirror lower fat quantity, and the reverse. The atlas fits all four markers in one model and asks, for each phenotype, whether density is associated with disease given area, and whether area is associated given density.

Of the 392 phenotypes with a joint signal, 130 had both area and density independently associated, 221 density only, 6 area only (hyperlipidaemia, disorders of lipoid metabolism, abnormal glucose, calculus of kidney, hypercholesterolaemia, abnormal mammogram/breast findings), and for 35 the signal was shared and could not be attributed to either. Among the 359 phenotypes with a significant density marker given area, higher density went with higher odds for 336 and lower odds for 23. The joint class was the same after adjusting for site and after excluding probable segmentation failures for 587 of 624 phenotypes.
